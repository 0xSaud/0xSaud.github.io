## 1. Study methodology: "Try Harder" vs. write-ups, building a personal methodology, notes, practising without Metasploit, adapting public exploits

### Takeaway
The common advice now is a balanced "Try Harder": time-box each practice box (often about 1.5 to 2 hours). Then look only at the hint for the step you are stuck on, not the full write-up. Write down what you missed, so every hint becomes part of your own methodology or checklist. Prepare by working through PEN-200 challenge labs (Secura, Relia, Medtech, OSCP A/B/C) and a community box list (TJ Null and/or LainKusanagi, mostly on Proving Grounds Practice). Practise mostly without Metasploit, because the exam allows Metasploit on only one target.

### Cited Findings
- **[CONSENSUS] Time-box, then take a targeted hint.** One piece of advice: time yourself on practice boxes. If a box takes more than about 1:30 to 2 hours, you may not have a concept down yet. Another "rule" from the same results: avoid hints and write-ups until you have truly given up, because "it is better to fail 10 times in practice than once in the exam" (snippet; the search results did not say exactly which of these pages it came from) — [Olivier Konate, "Lessons Learned From OSCP+"](https://olivierkonate.medium.com/lessons-learned-from-oscp-f988f00a49a8); [weifeng.c01, "I Passed OSCP: My Journey & Lessons Learnt"](https://medium.com/@weifeng.c01/i-passed-oscp-my-journey-lessons-learnt-eea002d79c99); [Robert Scocca, "Avoiding common OSCP pitfalls"](https://robertscocca.medium.com/avoiding-common-oscp-pitfalls-d2dd929fdb03)
- **[CONSENSUS] Use only the hint for the stuck step.** If you have been stuck for a long time, read the write-up for that one step only, not the whole box, then close it and carry on alone. It is fine to look at hints to learn an attack vector, "but if you make that a habit, you will suffer in the exam" (snippet) — [Kirkian Computing, "OSCP: Try Harder"](https://kirkiancomputing.co.uk/2023/03/20/oscp-try-harder/); [Schellman, "Conquering the OSCP: Self-Study Tips and Exam Tricks"](https://www.schellman.com/blog/penetration-testing/conquering-the-oscp-self-study-tips-and-exam-tricks); [OffSec blog, "9 OSCP Study Tips"](https://www.offsec.com/blog/oscp-study-tips-to-help-you-succeed/)
- **[SINGLE, fetched] Hints are fine if you record why.** "Hints and walkthroughs are acceptable if you document methodology and reasoning." The author says you are ready "when you can start a box and run through your enumeration like a well-oiled machine and identify potential vectors for investigation." When stuck: "review notes before external research" — [OoStellarnightoO/OSCP_Notes (GitHub, OSCP+ passed early 2025)](https://github.com/OoStellarnightoO/OSCP_Notes)
- **[SINGLE, fetched] Example 6-week plan (OSCP+, Jan 2025).** Weeks 2–4: PEN-200 exercises, with a focus on selected modules, plus a Proving Grounds subscription. Week 5: challenge labs Secura, Relia, Medtech, OSCP A/B/C, and starting PG boxes. Week 6: the remaining practice boxes, and redoing the labs **without hints**. "PWK materials alone are insufficient; supplement with TryHackMe, HackTheBox, TCM Academy." — [OoStellarnightoO/OSCP_Notes](https://github.com/OoStellarnightoO/OSCP_Notes)
- **[SINGLE, fetched] Note-taking tool.** Obsidian with community plugins, using Templater to drop in an enumeration template for each box, synced through cloud storage — [OoStellarnightoO/OSCP_Notes](https://github.com/OoStellarnightoO/OSCP_Notes)
- **[CONSENSUS] Box lists: TJ Null + LainKusanagi, Proving Grounds first.** Many candidates combine the TJ Null and LainKusanagi lists and do only Proving Grounds (Play/Practice) boxes, which are often preferred over HackTheBox for OSCP prep. Advice: "pick one list, commit to it" (snippet) — [Blu3ming, "Beyond Try Harder: My OSCP Preparation Journey"](https://blu3ming.github.io/oscp-preparation-journey/); [Ch4os1/OSCP-Exam-Lab-Prep-List (GitHub)](https://github.com/Ch4os1/OSCP-Exam-Lab-Prep-List); [equilibriumsecurity, "My OSCP Experience"](https://equilibriumsecurity.substack.com/p/my-oscp-experience-tips-and-suggestions)
- **[SINGLE] Volume target.** "Aim for at least 30–40 rooted machines before taking your exam" and "Don't rely on a tool, build a methodology instead" (snippet) — [HackerDNA, "OSCP+ Roadmap 2026"](https://hackerdna.com/blog/oscp-preparation-guide) / [AceFortis, "OSCP Certification: How to Pass Exam in 2026"](https://acefortis.com/2026/02/26/oscp-certification-guide-how-to-pass-2026/). An Arabic Saudi training-institute article gives 40–50 lab machines — [Masartech (Arabic)](https://masartech.com.sa/%F0%9F%94%8D-%D8%A7%D8%AE%D8%AA%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1-oscp-%D9%83%D9%8A%D9%81-%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%B2%D9%87-%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AD%D8%A7%D9%88%D9%84%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%88/)
- **[SINGLE] Rework hard boxes.** Do as many challenge-lab machines as you can, exploit a machine in more than one way, and go back to the ones you found hard. "The OSCP exam really sticks to what's in PEN-200 and the challenge labs" (snippet) — [duckwrites, "Conquering Active Directory for OSCP+ — Part 3"](https://duckwrites.medium.com/conquering-active-directory-for-oscp-essential-techniques-and-strategies-part-3-a719c6b81ad8)
- **[OFFICIAL] Why to practise without Metasploit.** You may use Metasploit modules (Auxiliary, Exploit, Post) or the Meterpreter payload against **one single target machine** of your choice. Once you use it on a target, that choice is locked in, even if the attack fails (snippet of the official guide) — [OffSec OSCP+ Exam Guide](https://help.offsec.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040165632-OSCP-Exam-Guide)
- **[SINGLE] Balance Linux and Windows/AD practice.** One candidate failed because they practised "too much on Linux machines when the exam required Windows and AD knowledge" (snippet) — [ggk570577, "How Not to Fail the OSCP+ Exam"](https://medium.com/@ggk570577/how-not-to-fail-the-exam-lessons-from-my-oscp-journey-3d951a3c9e0b) / [zeroDaykt, "Mastering OSCP+ in 2025–26"](https://medium.com/@zeroDaykt/mastering-oscp-in-2025-26-the-updated-exam-my-fails-wins-how-you-can-do-it-c44534bfcf54)
- **[SINGLE] Build your own AD lab.** Hosting your own AD lab "from scratch" was described as a turning point (snippet) — same failure-lessons results as above ([cybermindspace, "I Failed My OSCP Exam 3 Times Before Passing"](https://medium.com/@cybermindspace/i-failed-my-oscp-exam-3-times-before-passing-heres-what-finally-worked-442e52e562a1))
- **[SINGLE] Learn more from mistakes.** One candidate "learned more from mistakes than from quickly getting flags" (snippet) — [InfoSec Write-ups, "The OSCP is a mental game"](https://infosecwriteups.com/the-oscp-is-a-mental-game-d6583fc6b20b?gi=398b46ee9dde)

### Inferences
- The old pure "Try Harder" (never look at write-ups) has largely been replaced by **time-box, then take a narrow hint, then write down the lesson**. Sources disagree only on how long the time-box should be. "Fully surrendered" and "about 1.5 to 2 hours" are both quoted, and no exact number is consensus.
- The point of reading hints is to turn every hint into a new line in a personal checklist. Several passers tie readiness to having a repeatable enumeration routine, not to a box count.
- The one-target Metasploit rule means candidates need manual exploitation skills by default. That includes reading and changing public exploits (for example from Exploit-DB or GitHub) to fix IPs, ports, payloads and Python 2/3 issues. This last point is inferred from the rule. I did not find a 2024–2026 source in my results that teaches exploit modification as a lesson.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full r/oscp "passed/failed" threads (Reddit blocked), so I could not measure how widely each tip is repeated on Reddit.
- I found no 2024–2026 text source, in the pages I could open, that gives Tib3rius', TCM Security's, John Hammond's or IppSec's specific OSCP+ advice. Their content is mostly video. IppSec is commonly recommended for watching methodology, but I have no source for that in this research.
- I found no 2024–2026 source on "reading and modifying public exploits" as a study topic.

## 2. Enumeration methodology (port scanning, web, SMB/LDAP/SNMP, AD, rabbit holes)

### Takeaway
"If you're stuck, you haven't enumerated enough" is the most repeated line. The standard approach: scan all TCP ports with version and script detection, and always add a UDP scan (for SNMP on 161). Run two or more directory brute-force tools or wordlists, recursively. For the AD set, start with the given credentials and run BloodHound, Kerberoasting / AS-REP roasting and share/description checks right away. Escalate locally and loot each box, because it holds what you need for the next one. Time-box any single attack path to avoid rabbit holes.

### Cited Findings
- **[CONSENSUS] Full TCP scan.** Run `nmap -p- -sV -sC -v <IP> --open`. Verbose output lets you start enumerating open ports before the scan finishes (snippet) — [hackwithmike, "OSCP Methodology"](https://hackwithmike.gitbook.io/oscp/methodology/oscp-methodology); [shawn2600, "OSCP (PEN-200) Part 1 — Enumeration"](https://medium.com/@shawn2600/how-to-pass-the-2023-oscp-pen-200-on-the-first-try-part-1-enumeration-a0b272a86cf7)
- **[CONSENSUS] Don't skip UDP.** Vulnerable UDP services such as SNMP (161/162) can be the way in. A quick UDP scan of common ports is fast (snippet) — [hackwithmike, "OSCP Last Minute Tips"](https://hackwithmike.gitbook.io/oscp/methodology/oscp-last-minute-tips); [manish.tiwari, "My OSCP Preparation Notes: Port Enumeration Techniques"](https://medium.com/@manish.tiwari_68866/my-oscp-preparation-notes-port-enumeration-techniques-eb652e3097a1)
- **[SINGLE] Web content brute-forcing.** Use two or more tools and wordlists, for example feroxbuster and dirsearch with `raft-medium-directories.txt` and `directory-list-2.3-medium.txt`. Always brute-force recursively. Feroxbuster does this by default (snippet) — [hackwithmike, "OSCP Last Minute Tips"](https://hackwithmike.gitbook.io/oscp/methodology/oscp-last-minute-tips)
- **[CONSENSUS] Being stuck means more enumeration.** "If you're stuck, you haven't enumerated enough — re-run scans with different wordlists, check all ports again, and look at source code." The exam is described as having "intentionally misleading paths and dead ends (rabbit holes)" (snippet) — [hackwithmike](https://hackwithmike.gitbook.io/oscp/methodology/oscp-last-minute-tips); [pentestmindmap, "OSCP Cheatsheet 2026"](https://pentestmindmap.com/oscp-cheatsheet)
- **[SINGLE] Exploiting too early.** "The single biggest cause of OSCP failures is running exploits before finishing enumeration" (snippet; commercial cheat-sheet site, so an opinion) — [pentestmindmap](https://pentestmindmap.com/oscp-cheatsheet) / [examcert.app](https://www.examcert.app/blog/oscp-complete-guide/)
- **[SINGLE, fetched] Order after a shell.** Do manual enumeration first, then run automated tools (linPEAS/winPEAS-type). Form a hypothesis and "execute without hesitation" — [OoStellarnightoO/OSCP_Notes](https://github.com/OoStellarnightoO/OSCP_Notes)
- **[CONSENSUS] Rabbit-hole time limits.** Suggested limits vary: "more than 45 minutes without meaningful progress on a single attack path" means tunnel vision — [kioptrix.com, "OSCP Failure Stories"](https://kioptrix.com/oscp-failure-stories/) (snippet). "Don't spend more than 2 hours on a single machine; if stuck, move on and come back later" — [hackwithmike](https://hackwithmike.gitbook.io/oscp/methodology/oscp-last-minute-tips) (snippet).
- **[CONSENSUS] The AD set is "assumed breach".** You get valid domain-user credentials at the start. Work out each machine's role (DC or client) and its services, and find the easiest first target (snippet) — [OffSec, "OSCP+ Exam with AD Preparation"](https://help.offsec.com/hc/en-us/articles/4547917816468-OffSec-OSCP-Exam-with-AD-Preparation); [anshu19981, "OSCP+ Complete Exam Checklist & Attack Chains 2025-26"](https://anshu19981.github.io/OscpCheckList2026/)
- **[SINGLE] First steps with the given credentials.** Run BloodHound first. Check every user's description field for passwords ("very common in OSCP"). Check SYSVOL/GPP in the first 10 minutes. Kerberoast and AS-REP roast immediately (snippet) — [anshu19981 checklist](https://anshu19981.github.io/OscpCheckList2026/)
- **[CONSENSUS] Loot before moving on.** "Getting a low-priv shell on MS01 isn't the goal — it's the starting point. You need to escalate locally on MS01 first, then loot it properly, because the credentials/tickets that unlock MS02 are usually sitting on MS01." Post-exploitation matters as much as initial enumeration (snippet) — [anshu19981 checklist](https://anshu19981.github.io/OscpCheckList2026/); [duckwrites Part 3](https://duckwrites.medium.com/conquering-active-directory-for-oscp-essential-techniques-and-strategies-part-3-a719c6b81ad8)
- **[SINGLE, low confidence] ZeroLogon/noPac.** One checklist says ZeroLogon and noPac are "the two most common 'unauthenticated → Domain Admin' AD chains in current OSCP-style labs" (snippet). This conflicts with the assumed-breach design and is not backed by other sources. Treat it as unverified — [anshu19981 checklist](https://anshu19981.github.io/OscpCheckList2026/)
- **[SINGLE] Keep an AD cheat sheet.** "Have a cheatsheet of AD commands" (snippet) — [duckwrites Part 3](https://duckwrites.medium.com/conquering-active-directory-for-oscp-essential-techniques-and-strategies-part-3-a719c6b81ad8)
- **[SINGLE] Pivoting and port-forwarding.** Retakers practised pivoting and port-forwarding (for example ligolo-ng/chisel-style tunnels) for the internal AD machines (snippet; the tool names are my examples, not from the source) — [ggk570577](https://medium.com/@ggk570577/how-not-to-fail-the-exam-lessons-from-my-oscp-journey-3d951a3c9e0b)

### Inferences
- Read together, the advice says AD failures are usually a post-exploitation and looting problem, not an initial-access problem. Candidates get MS01 but don't escalate and harvest credentials, so they can't reach MS02 or the DC.
- The sources were consistent on scanning (`nmap -p-` plus a UDP top-ports scan). Rustscan is often mentioned in the community as a fast first pass, but none of the sources I could reach recommended it. Present it as optional.
- Rabbit-hole time-boxes run from 45 minutes per attack path to 2 hours per machine. A reasonable synthesis is a short limit per vector and a longer one per box, then rotate to another target.

### Gaps
- Specific SMB/LDAP enumeration commands (netexec/crackmapexec, enum4linux-ng, ldapsearch, smbclient) were not in the snippets I got. They are standard in cheat sheets, but I have no 2024–2026 "passer" source for them in this research.
- I could not verify the ZeroLogon/noPac claim, or confirm whether the exam's AD set ever relies on such CVEs. It probably doesn't, given the assumed-breach design and OffSec's policy, but this is unconfirmed.

## 3. Exam-day strategy for the current OSCP+ format (order, point combinations, breaks/sleep, proctoring, proofs, when stuck)

### Takeaway
The current exam (since 1 Nov 2024) has **no bonus points**. You get 23h45m of hacking plus 24 hours to write the report. There is one AD set worth 40 points, now scored per machine at 10/10/20, and three standalone machines worth 20 points each (10 for local.txt, 10 for proof.txt). You need 70 to pass. The most cited plan is to do the AD set early while fresh. Some do one standalone first as a warm-up. The usual target is AD 40 + one full standalone (20) + one foothold (10). Proof screenshots must show the flag **and** the IP (ipconfig/ifconfig) in the same interactive shell. AI/LLM tools are banned, and Metasploit is limited to one target.

### Cited Findings
- **[CONSENSUS/OFFICIAL] OSCP+ start date and bonus points.** OSCP+ was introduced by OffSec on **1 November 2024** (snippet) — [Wikipedia, "Offensive Security Certified Professional"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offensive_Security_Certified_Professional). "The old '10 bonus points for 80% lab completion' scheme ended with the Nov 2024 OSCP+ format change — 70/100 must be earned live on exam day" (snippet) — [HackerDNA](https://hackerdna.com/blog/oscp-preparation-guide)
- **[CONSENSUS] Timing.** A 23-hour 45-minute practical exam, then 24 hours to write and submit the report (snippet) — [HackerDNA](https://hackerdna.com/blog/oscp-preparation-guide); [Certsqill exam guide](https://www.certsqill.com/exam-guides/oscp); [CertCrush 12-week plan](https://www.certcrush.app/blog/how-to-pass-oscp-2026-12-week-study-plan)
- **[CONSENSUS] Scoring.** AD set 40 points "awarded per machine in the set (10, 10 and 20), so a chain that stalls halfway still scores, unlike the old all-or-nothing AD set". Three standalones at 20 points each (10 local.txt + 10 proof.txt). Pass mark 70. "The full AD set (40) plus one full standalone (20) and one foothold (10) is exactly 70" (snippet) — [HackerDNA](https://hackerdna.com/blog/oscp-preparation-guide); [Certsqill](https://www.certsqill.com/exam-guides/oscp); [AceFortis](https://acefortis.com/2026/02/26/oscp-certification-guide-how-to-pass-2026/). **Conflict:** one search summary listed the standalones as "10, 20, 25 points". That seems wrong. It is unsupported and contradicts the other sources and the 20/20/20 structure — [search snippet tied to WebAsha / AceFortis results](https://www.webasha.com/blog/10-tips-to-ace-the-pen-200-oscp-and-oscp-certification-exam-in-1st-attempt-complete-guide)
- **[CONSENSUS] Order of attack.** "Because the AD set is worth 40 points, tackle it while you are still mentally fresh. Multiple successful candidates recommend doing one standalone first, then moving to the AD set, rather than grinding standalones into burnout before touching AD" (snippet) — [Aryan, "How I Passed the OSCP in 2026"](https://medium.com/@contact.aryan.here/oscp-exam-2026-strategy-reporting-tips-and-lessons-484363f29f6c); [2O0K, "How I passed OSCP+ in two months"](https://medium.com/@2O0K/how-i-passed-oscp-in-two-months-14685a324e83)
- **[SINGLE] AD pace benchmark.** "If you are following the correct methodology, you should be able to gain the initial foothold and the second AD flag within 2–3 hours" (snippet) — [AceFortis](https://acefortis.com/2026/02/26/oscp-certification-guide-how-to-pass-2026/) / [astikrawat, "OSCP+: Step-by-Step Guide to Success"](https://astikrawat.medium.com/oscp-step-by-step-guide-to-success-9ff3d189dbb2)
- **[SINGLE] Alternative 70-point route.** "40 points from Full AD, 20 points from Easy standalone box, 10 points from Medium box" (snippet) — [HackerDNA](https://hackerdna.com/blog/oscp-preparation-guide)
- **[OFFICIAL] Metasploit rule.** Metasploit/Meterpreter may be used against only one target. You can't try it on several machines before choosing, and if it fails on your chosen target you can't use it on a second one (snippet) — [OffSec OSCP+ Exam Guide](https://help.offsec.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040165632-OSCP-Exam-Guide)
- **[OFFICIAL] AI ban.** "Using LLMs and AI chatbots (OffSec KAI, ChatGPT, Deepseek, Gemini, etc.) is strictly prohibited". Use of KAI or other chatbots "is not allowed during the exam and the exam reporting phase" (snippet) — [OffSec OSCP+ Exam Guide](https://help.offsec.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040165632-OSCP-Exam-Guide); [OffSec OSCP+ Exam FAQ](https://help.offsec.com/hc/en-us/articles/4412170923924-OSCP-Exam-FAQ)
- **[OFFICIAL] Proof screenshots.** You prove success with screenshots and by submitting each proof file (local.txt / proof.txt) to the control panel. The proof.txt screenshot must show both the flag contents and the IP address (ifconfig/ipconfig) in the same terminal. "Two separate screenshots do not count." Not providing the proof files in a screenshot for each machine means zero points for that target. Reading the proof files any other way, "including any type of web-based shell", also means zero points (snippet) — [OffSec OSCP+ Exam Guide](https://help.offsec.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040165632-OSCP-Exam-Guide); [PentestReportAI, "OSCP Report Template (2026 OSCP+)"](https://www.pentestreportai.com/blog/oscp-report-template)
- **[SINGLE] Breaks when stuck.** "Take a big break if you feel stuck". Do something away from the screen (exercise, eat) and come back fresh. "The exam rewards patience, not panic" (snippet) — [InfoSec Write-ups, "The OSCP is a mental game"](https://infosecwriteups.com/the-oscp-is-a-mental-game-d6583fc6b20b?gi=398b46ee9dde)
- **[SINGLE, fetched] Luck matters.** "If you fail OSCP, it may not be because you are unprepared, but because you faced very hard boxes or had terrible exam environment luck" — [OoStellarnightoO/OSCP_Notes](https://github.com/OoStellarnightoO/OSCP_Notes)
- **[SINGLE] Pre-exam game plan.** There is a dedicated "Last Minute Checks/Timelines/Game Plan Before OSCP Exam" write-up (title only; I could not read the content) — [TheHuskyHacker](https://medium.com/@TheHuskyHacker/last-minute-checks-timelines-game-plan-before-oscp-exam-0045ee2689e3)

### Inferences
- **All combinations that reach 70** under 10/10/20 AD partial scoring plus 3×(10+10) standalones (my own arithmetic from the scoring above):
  - AD 40 + 30 from standalones (one full standalone + one foothold, or three footholds).
  - AD 20 (two machines) + 50 from standalones (two full + one foothold).
  - AD 10 (first machine only) + 60 (all three standalones fully rooted).
  - AD 0 cannot pass, because the standalones top out at 60. **So at least some AD progress is required to pass.** This is a strong reason why the sources stress AD.
- So "AD first, or one standalone then AD" works out to the same rule: secure the 40 points while fresh, then pick up standalone footholds. Low-privilege footholds (10 each) are often quicker than root on a hard box.
- Practical exam-day habits that follow from the proof rules: take the proof screenshot as soon as you have the flag, from an interactive shell (not a web shell), with `type proof.txt` / `cat proof.txt` and `ipconfig` / `ip a` in the same window. Submit the flag in the control panel right away. Collect screenshots of every step as you go, because the report can't be supplemented later (see section 5).

### Gaps
- **Proctoring details** (webcam/screen-share requirements, ID check, check-in time before the start, break rules, allowed number of monitors) are in OffSec's Proctoring FAQ, which I could not open. I found no 2024–2026 passer source on proctoring tips in what I could reach. The report writer should take these from OffSec's official Proctoring FAQ, not from this note.
- **Sleep scheduling:** I found no specific, citable 2024–2026 sleep schedule. The general "take a big break" advice is cited above. Detailed timelines (for example "sleep after hour X") were in pages I couldn't open (TheHuskyHacker, Aryan).
- I could not verify from the official page itself that the AD partial scoring is exactly 10/10/20 (I only have consistent third-party snippets). The official exam guide should be checked before the final report.

## 4. Most common reasons people fail, and how retakers changed their approach

### Takeaway
The failure reasons that keep coming up are: (1) rabbit holes and tunnel vision, (2) weak Windows/AD and post-exploitation (pivoting, looting), (3) unbalanced practice (Linux-heavy, course-reading-heavy, little lab work), (4) incomplete enumeration, and (5) documentation or proof mistakes that cost points. Retakers fixed these by shifting to Windows/AD, building their own AD labs, finishing a community box list (TJ Null) on PG, practising pivoting, and making report writing routine.

### Cited Findings
- **[CONSENSUS] Unbalanced practice.** One failure "wasn't because they hadn't practiced enough boxes — it was because their practice was unbalanced, focusing too much on Linux machines when the exam required Windows and AD knowledge" (snippet) — [ggk570577, "How Not to Fail the OSCP+ Exam"](https://medium.com/@ggk570577/how-not-to-fail-the-exam-lessons-from-my-oscp-journey-3d951a3c9e0b); [zeroDaykt, "Mastering OSCP+ in 2025–26: My Fails, Wins"](https://medium.com/@zeroDaykt/mastering-oscp-in-2025-26-the-updated-exam-my-fails-wins-how-you-can-do-it-c44534bfcf54)
- **[CONSENSUS] Rabbit holes.** Candidates got "fixated on rabbit holes, chasing enticing vectors and overlooking larger patterns in enumeration output". More than 45 minutes without progress on one path is "tunnel-vision hacking" (snippet) — [kioptrix.com, "OSCP Failure Stories: 7 Brutal Lessons"](https://kioptrix.com/oscp-failure-stories/); [anezaneo, "OSCP Fail: From 'Try Harder' to 'Read Slower'"](https://medium.com/@anezaneo/oscp-fail-from-try-harder-to-read-slower-how-it-rewired-my-pentest-game-c267efdf2af5)

## 5. Report writing (templates, required content, rejection causes)

### Takeaway
OffSec expects a professional, reproducible PDF that documents every step, command and console output, with local.txt/proof.txt contents and the proof screenshots. The free, widely used tools are **SysReptor's OffSec templates** (cloud or self-hosted, OSCP+ supported) and **noraj's Markdown → PDF template** (Pandoc + Eisvogel, with 7z/MD5 packaging). Reports are rejected or lose points for: proof screenshots without the IP in the same window, proof obtained through a web shell, missing steps or screenshots (which can't be added after submission), and using AI during the reporting phase.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL] What to document.** "You must document all of your attacks including all steps, commands issued, and console output in the form of a penetration test report". The contents of local.txt and proof.txt must be included in the report (snippet) — [OffSec OSCP+ Exam Guide](https://help.offsec.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040165632-OSCP-Exam-Guide)
- **[CONSENSUS] Report sections.** "Overall High-Level Summary and Recommendations (non-technical), Methodology walkthrough and detailed outline of steps taken, Each finding with included screenshots, walkthrough, sample code, and proof.txt if applicable". OffSec "does not mandate a strict template but expects a professional, reproducible report in PDF format… thorough enough that a third-party reader could replicate your attacks step-by-step" (snippet) — [PentestReportAI](https://www.pentestreportai.com/blog/oscp-report-template); [Pentellect, "OSCP Report Template: What to Include"](https://pentellect.hashnode.dev/oscp-report-template); a sample of the official structure — [SysReptor sample OSCP report (PDF)](https://docs.sysreptor.com/assets/reports/OffSec-OSCP-Exam-Report.pdf)
- **[fetched] noraj template.** Markdown templates for OffSec exam reports (OSCP and many others) that "eliminate the need for LaTeX, Microsoft Word, or LibreOffice Writer". It includes the official OffSec templates plus community-improved versions (whoisflynn OSCP v3.2). PDFs are produced with Pandoc + Eisvogel, and it "creates 7z archives with MD5 hash verification for submission compliance". About 4.2k GitHub stars — [noraj/OSCP-Exam-Report-Template-Markdown](https://github.com/noraj/OSCP-Exam-Report-Template-Markdown); [example output PDF](https://noraj.github.io/OSCP-Exam-Report-Template-Markdown/output/examples/OSCP-exam-report-template_OS_v2.pdf)
- **[fetched] SysReptor OffSec Reporting.** Free (cloud and self-hosted). Explicitly supports **OSCP+** and other OffSec certifications. Write in Markdown, render to PDF in one click. Templates follow "official OffSec reports". The tagline is "Focus on hacking, not the formatting" — [Syslifters/OffSec-Reporting (GitHub)](https://github.com/Syslifters/OffSec-Reporting); cloud sign-up — [oscp.sysreptor.com](https://oscp.sysreptor.com/)
- **[OFFICIAL] Causes of lost points or rejection.** (a) A proof.txt screenshot without the IP (ipconfig/ifconfig) in the same terminal: "two separate screenshots do not count". (b) No proof-file screenshot per machine means zero points for it. (c) Proofs obtained through "any type of web-based shell" mean zero points. (d) "Failure to provide sufficient documentation will result in reduced or zero points". (e) The submission is final, and missing screenshots can't be sent later (snippet) — [OffSec OSCP+ Exam Guide](https://help.offsec.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040165632-OSCP-Exam-Guide); [PentestReportAI](https://www.pentestreportai.com/blog/oscp-report-template)
- **[OFFICIAL] No AI in the report.** AI chatbots (including OffSec KAI) are banned "during the exam and the exam reporting phase" (snippet) — [OffSec OSCP+ Exam Guide](https://help.offsec.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040165632-OSCP-Exam-Guide)
- **[SINGLE] Practise reporting.** Report writing should "feel like muscle memory" before the exam (snippet) — [cybermindspace](https://medium.com/@cybermindspace/i-failed-my-oscp-exam-3-times-before-passing-heres-what-finally-worked-442e52e562a1)
- **[OUTDATED] "The report is half the grade."** An Arabic training-institute article says the report "represents half the exam grade". That is wrong for any OSCP version. Points come from flags, and the report only *validates* them (and can cost points) — [Masartech (Arabic)](https://masartech.com.sa/%D9%83%D9%8A%D9%81-%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%87%D8%AC%D9%88%D9%85%D9%8A%D8%9F-%D8%A3%D8%B3%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%AF%D9%88%D8%B1%D8%A9-oscp-%D8%A7/) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- Since a submitted report is final and missing screenshots can't be added later, the passers' habit of screenshotting every step during the exam (not afterwards) matters more than which template you use.
- A good workflow (inferred from the tools above) is to set up a SysReptor or noraj project before the exam, fill in findings during exam breaks, and use the 24-hour reporting window only for review and packaging.

### Gaps
- The exact submission rules (file naming such as `OSCP-OS-XXXXX-Exam-Report.pdf`, the .7z archive requirement, the upload portal, the 24-hour deadline wording) could not be checked against the official page in this session. The report writer should confirm them on the [OffSec OSCP+ Exam Guide](https://help.offsec.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040165632-OSCP-Exam-Guide).
- I found no first-hand 2024–2026 account of a report being rejected. All the rejection causes come from the official rules and secondary summaries.
